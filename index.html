2. FURTHER CLASSROOM IMPLICATION — WHAT WILL I CHANGE?

A. Listening before reading

Create opportunities to hear, discriminate and manipulate sounds orally before showing the written form.

B. Make sound–symbol relationships explicit

Prompt children to look at letters, say sounds, blend and check meaning rather than guess from pictures.

C. Build blending and segmenting into daily practice

Use short, purposeful routines for blending, segmenting, decoding and encoding.

D. Move from decoding to meaning

After decoding, ask children to reread, explain, connect and use the word or sentence.

E. Differentiate support

Identify whether difficulty occurs in hearing, identifying, blending, segmenting, decoding, encoding or understanding.

Further Classroom Implications

2. FURTHER CLASSROOM IMPLICATION — WHAT WILL I DO DIFFERENTLY?

A. Teach words as connected networks

Move beyond isolated vocabulary lists. Help children connect a new word to word families, synonyms, antonyms, related concepts, morphology and examples.

B. Make morphology visible

Use meaningful word parts to help learners unlock unfamiliar words, spelling patterns and related vocabulary.

C. Build contextual vocabulary

Introduce words in meaningful contexts, revisit them across texts and situations, and ask learners to explain, compare, choose and use them.

D. Develop word consciousness

Create a classroom where children notice words, ask about words, collect words, play with words and become curious about how language works.

E. Strengthen oral language through interaction

Use TALK → LISTEN → THINK → RESPOND through pair talk, discussion, drama, role play, questioning and purposeful response.

DAY 3 — READ & UNDERSTAND

FROM READING TO MEANING-MAKING

Further Classroom Implications

2. FURTHER CLASSROOM IMPLICATION — WHAT WILL I DO DIFFERENTLY?

A. Make reading an active thinking process

Reading should involve purposeful thinking before, during and after the text — not simply reading aloud and answering questions.

B. Use quality children's literature

Use picture books, stories, storytelling and read-alouds to develop vocabulary, imagination, comprehension, discussion and reading for pleasure.

C. Teach comprehension explicitly

Model and scaffold PREDICT → CONNECT → QUESTION → INFER → INTERPRET → SUMMARISE → RESPOND rather than assuming comprehension happens automatically.

D. Strengthen listening, speaking, reading and writing together

Give children opportunities to talk about a text, listen to others, formulate ideas, read for evidence and write or speak a response.

E. Extend reading across the curriculum

Help children read to learn in Gardening, Mathematics, TWAU, WE and other learning areas — using text, diagrams, instructions, problems, explanations and informational sources.
